







Agricatch
“A Web Application for Pre-Ordering Fresh Produce from Farmers for Trabajo Market ”










CHAPTER 1

PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND

Introduction 
The rapid advancement of digital technology has greatly influenced how we access agricultural and fishery products today. I have observed that digital platforms now offer more convenient, efficient, and transparent ways for consumers to connect with sources of fresh goods. These innovations have reshaped traditional buying practices and introduced new opportunities for improving food distribution systems. However, despite these technological improvements, not all sectors have equally benefited from digital transformation.
From my perspective, many local farmers and fishermen in the Philippines continue to face significant challenges. Limited market access, inefficient distribution processes, and post-harvest losses remain common issues that directly affect their livelihoods. These problems often result in unstable and unpredictable income, making it difficult for producers to sustain their operations. At the same time, I have noticed that consumers also experience difficulties, such as fluctuating prices, inconsistent product quality, and limited availability of fresh agricultural and fishery products.
I have seen these challenges reflected in Trabajo Market, a community-based wet market in Manila. The market largely relies on estimated demand rather than confirmed orders, as there is no structured pre-ordering system in place. Because of this, vendors frequently encounter situations of oversupply or undersupply, which leads to food waste, financial losses, and inconvenience for both sellers and buyers seeking fresh and affordable goods.
Recognizing these concerns, I propose AgriCatch as a web-based application designed to support the pre-ordering of fresh agricultural and fishery products. This platform aims to directly connect farmers and fishermen with vendors and consumers, creating a more organized and predictable supply chain. Through better coordination, I believe the system can help maintain product freshness, minimize waste, and improve overall efficiency in market transactions.
Overall, this initiative reflects my broader goal of exploring how digital solutions can strengthen traditional wet markets. By integrating technology into local food systems, I aim to promote fairer income opportunities for producers, more reliable access for consumers, and a more sustainable approach to food distribution. This study therefore examines the acceptability and effectiveness of AgriCatch as a practical digital innovation for community-based markets.


















Theoretical Framework
This study is guided by several theories that explain how digital technology can improve agricultural and fishery market systems, particularly in traditional wet markets. I use these theories to understand how a web-based pre-ordering platform such as AgriCatch can influence supply chain coordination, user acceptability, and overall market efficiency.
First, I anchor this study on Supply Chain Management (SCM) Theory, which emphasizes the importance of coordination, information sharing, and demand forecasting among supply chain participants. In my observation, traditional wet markets often rely on estimated demand, resulting in oversupply, undersupply, and post-harvest losses. Guided by SCM theory, I view AgriCatch as a tool that can improve communication between farmers, fishermen, vendors, and consumers by allowing pre-orders. This coordination enables better planning, reduces waste, and contributes to more stable income for producers.
Second, I apply the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) to examine the acceptability of AgriCatch among users. According to TAM, perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use influence an individual’s willingness to adopt a new technology. In this study, I assess whether vendors and consumers find AgriCatch helpful in accessing fresh products and whether they find the system easy to use. I assume that higher perceived usefulness and ease of use will lead to greater acceptance of the platform.
Third, this study is supported by the Information and Communication Technology for Development (ICT4D) framework, which highlights the role of digital technology in empowering marginalized sectors. I use this framework to explain how AgriCatch can support local farmers and fishermen by improving their access to markets and reducing reliance on intermediaries. Through digital pre-ordering, I believe producers can participate more directly in market transactions, promoting transparency, inclusivity, and fair income opportunities.
In addition, I draw from Market Efficiency Theory, which suggests that markets function more efficiently when accurate and timely information is available. I apply this theory to explain how AgriCatch can reduce information gaps related to demand, pricing, and supply. By providing real-time pre-order data, the platform helps vendors and producers make informed decisions, minimize losses, and stabilize product availability in the market.
Lastly, I incorporate Sustainable Food System Theory to frame the broader impact of this study. This theory emphasizes reducing food waste, supporting local food producers, and ensuring consistent access to fresh and nutritious food. I view AgriCatch as a system that encourages planned harvesting and distribution, thereby reducing spoilage and contributing to environmental and economic sustainability.
Overall, these theories collectively guide my evaluation of the acceptability and effectiveness of AgriCatch as a digital solution for traditional wet markets. They provide a strong foundation for understanding how digital platforms can enhance coordination, improve livelihoods, and promote sustainability within local food systems.
















Statement of the Problem
Despite the continuous growth of digital technology, I have observed that many traditional wet markets in the Philippines still depend on manual and unorganized systems for selling and distributing agricultural and fishery products. This reliance on outdated practices often results in poor coordination between supply and demand. As a consequence, farmers, fishermen, vendors, and consumers experience issues such as post-harvest losses, unstable income, food waste, and difficulty in accessing fresh and affordable goods.
In the case of Trabajo Market in Manila, the absence of a structured pre-ordering system has led to transactions that are based mainly on estimated demand rather than confirmed orders. I noticed that this practice frequently causes oversupply or shortages of products, negatively affecting producers’ earnings and limiting vendors’ ability to consistently provide quality and fresh products to consumers. These inefficiencies highlight the need for a more organized and reliable market system.
Although digital solutions have been successfully used to improve coordination and efficiency in other industries, their application in traditional wet markets remains limited. This gap motivated me to explore the potential of a web-based pre-ordering system like AgriCatch, which aims to address existing market challenges by improving coordination, reducing waste, and supporting more stable income opportunities for local producers.
Specifically, this study aims to answer the following questions:
1. What challenges do farmers, fishermen, vendors, and consumers experience in the current transaction process at Trabajo Market?
2. How acceptable is the AgriCatch system in terms of usability, functionality, and convenience?
3. How effective is AgriCatch in improving supply chain coordination and product availability?
4. What perceived benefits does AgriCatch offer in reducing food waste and income instability?
5. What recommendations can be proposed to further improve the system for traditional wet markets?
Significance of the Study

This study is significant as it explores the potential of a digital solution to address persistent challenges in traditional wet markets, particularly in the distribution of agricultural and fishery products. By evaluating the acceptability and effectiveness of the AgriCatch system, this research contributes to a better understanding of how technology can improve market coordination, reduce inefficiencies, and promote sustainable practices within community-based markets.
For farmers and fishermen, this study is important because it highlights a platform that can provide more predictable demand, reduce post-harvest losses, and support fair and stable income opportunities. Through a structured pre-ordering system, producers may better plan harvesting and supply, allowing them to minimize waste and improve their livelihoods.
For vendors and consumers, the findings of this study may lead to more efficient transactions and improved access to fresh and quality products. Vendors can benefit from better inventory management, while consumers may experience more stable prices, improved product availability, and greater convenience when purchasing agricultural and fishery goods.
This study is also significant for market administrators and policymakers, as it provides insights into how digital tools can be integrated into traditional wet markets. The results may serve as a basis for developing policies, programs, or initiatives that support digital transformation in local food systems.
Lastly, this research contributes to future researchers and developers by providing empirical evidence on the application of digital platforms in wet markets. It may serve as a reference for further studies related to agricultural technology, supply chain digitalization, and sustainable food systems in similar community-based settings.



Scope and Delimitation

This study focuses on the evaluation of AgriCatch, a web-based pre-ordering application designed for the distribution of fresh agricultural and fishery products within Trabajo Market in Manila. The scope of the research covers the assessment of the system’s acceptability and effectiveness among selected market stakeholders, particularly vendors and consumers who are directly involved in daily market transactions.
The study primarily examines the acceptability of AgriCatch in terms of usability, functionality, convenience, and overall user experience. It also evaluates the effectiveness of the system in improving supply coordination, reducing oversupply and undersupply, and supporting access to fresh products. Data for the study are gathered through surveys and feedback from respondents who have interacted with or evaluated the proposed system.
This research is limited to the use of AgriCatch as a web-based platform and does not include mobile application development or integration with external e-commerce or payment systems. The study also does not measure long-term economic impacts, profit margins, or actual income changes of farmers and fishermen. Instead, it focuses on perceived effectiveness and user acceptance within the duration of the study.
Furthermore, the research is confined to a single wet market, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other markets with different structures or operational practices. Factors such as logistics outside the market, government regulations, internet infrastructure, and external market competition are not included in the scope of this study.






Definition of Terms

1. Digital Technology – Refers to electronic tools, systems, and platforms that facilitate information processing, communication, and transactions, such as web applications and online marketplaces.
2. Digital Platform – A software-based system that connects producers and consumers to enable transactions, ordering, and information sharing, like AgriCatch.
3. Agricultural Products – Fresh produce cultivated from farms, such as vegetables, fruits, and grains, intended for sale and consumption.
4. Fishery Products – Products sourced from fishing activities, including fish, shellfish, and other seafood sold in wet markets.
5. Wet Market – A traditional market where fresh food products, particularly agricultural and fishery goods, are sold directly to consumers.
6. Trabajo Market – A community-based wet market in Manila that serves as the primary study site for evaluating AgriCatch.
7. Pre-Ordering System – A process or platform that allows buyers to place orders for goods before they are delivered, improving supply chain planning.
8. AgriCatch – A proposed web-based application designed to facilitate the pre-ordering of agricultural and fishery products, directly connecting producers to vendors and consumers.
9. Supply Chain Coordination – The process of synchronizing production, distribution, and sales activities to ensure that products are delivered efficiently and meet market demand.
10. Post-Harvest Loss – Losses in quantity or quality of agricultural and fishery products that occur after harvest due to spoilage, inefficiency, or improper handling.
11. Market Access – The ability of farmers and fishermen to sell their products to consumers or vendors effectively, either physically or digitally.
12. Efficiency – The degree to which resources, such as time, labor, and products, are optimally used to achieve minimal waste and maximum output.
13. Transparency – The openness and clarity of information regarding product availability, pricing, and transactions in the supply chain.
14. User Acceptability – The willingness of vendors and consumers to adopt and use AgriCatch based on its perceived usefulness and ease of use.
15. Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) – A theoretical framework that explains how perceived usefulness and ease of use influence technology adoption.
16. Information and Communication Technology for Development (ICT4D) – A theory that emphasizes using digital tools to empower marginalized sectors and improve access to information and markets.
17. Market Efficiency – The condition in which market prices and product availability reflect accurate and timely information, allowing informed decisions.
18. Sustainable Food System – A system that ensures the production, distribution, and consumption of food in ways that minimize waste, protect the environment, and support fair livelihoods.
19. Oversupply – A situation where the quantity of products available exceeds the market demand, often leading to waste and financial losses.
20. Undersupply – A situation where the quantity of products available is insufficient to meet market demand, causing shortages and consumer dissatisfaction.








CHAPTER 2

REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDY

This chapter presents related literature and studies that support the development of AgriCatch, a web-based pre-ordering system for agricultural and fishery products in traditional wet markets. The reviewed sources focus on digital technology in agriculture, supply chain management, technology acceptance, ICT for development, market efficiency, sustainability, and Philippine-based digital agriculture initiatives.

Related Literature
Digital Technology in Agriculture and Fisheries
According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (2019), digital technologies improve access to agricultural markets by enabling faster communication, better coordination, and accurate information on supply and demand. These technologies help farmers and fishermen reduce post-harvest losses and improve income stability.
According to the World Bank (2020), digital platforms increase transparency in food distribution systems by allowing producers to connect directly with buyers. This reduces reliance on intermediaries and improves efficiency in agricultural markets.
According to Briones, Galang, and Latigar (2023), digital technology has strong potential to transform Philippine agri-food systems by improving productivity and market access. However, the authors emphasized that inclusive digital platforms are necessary to ensure that small farmers and fisherfolk benefit from digital transformation.



Supply Chain Management and Pre-Ordering Systems
According to Chopra and Meindl (2016), effective supply chain management depends on coordination, information sharing, and accurate demand forecasting. Poor coordination often leads to oversupply, shortages, and food waste, which are common in traditional wet markets.
According to Kurnia et al. (2015), electronic marketplaces and pre-ordering systems allow sellers to plan production based on confirmed orders, reducing spoilage and inventory losses. This supports the purpose of AgriCatch as a demand-driven system.

Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
According to Davis (1989), the Technology Acceptance Model explains that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use influence users’ willingness to adopt a system. Users are more likely to accept technology if it improves their tasks and is easy to operate.
According to Tarhini et al. (2017), users show higher acceptance of web-based systems when they perceive them as beneficial and convenient. This theory supports the evaluation of AgriCatch’s usability and functionality among vendors and consumers.
According to Summers (2023), Filipino farmers in Cavite were more willing to adopt e-commerce platforms when they perceived them as useful, easy to use, and helpful in improving sales. This confirms the relevance of TAM in the Philippine agricultural context.

Information and Communication Technology for Development (ICT4D)
According to Heeks (2018), ICT for Development focuses on using digital tools to empower marginalized groups such as farmers and fishermen by improving access to markets and information.
According to the Asian Development Bank (2021), digital agriculture platforms strengthen local food systems in Asia by providing producers with real-time data and better selling opportunities.
According to the Department of Agriculture (2024), the Philippine government continues to promote digital agricultural initiatives to modernize farming and improve market coordination, supporting the adoption of systems like AgriCatch.

Market Efficiency and Sustainable Food Systems
According to Fama (1970), markets function more efficiently when participants have access to timely and accurate information. Digital platforms reduce information gaps related to supply, demand, and pricing.
According to the United Nations Environment Programme (2021), food waste can be reduced through better planning and demand-based distribution systems. Pre-ordering platforms help minimize excess supply and product spoilage.
According to Belen (2019), Filipino farmers and consumers show increasing awareness of digital marketplaces for agricultural products, but acceptance depends on system design, logistics, and ease of use.

Related Studies
According to Reardon et al. (2019), digital food distribution systems in developing countries improve supply predictability and reduce income instability among small producers.
According to Lim and Sia (2020), online food ordering systems improve inventory management and operational efficiency, resulting in reduced waste and improved service.
According to Dela Cruz, Ramos, and Santos (2021), digital platforms in the Philippines improve farmers’ market access and reduce dependence on middlemen, leading to more stable income opportunities.
According to Ken Research (2025), the Philippine digital agriculture market is growing due to increased technology adoption, government support, and demand for efficient supply chain solutions.


Synthesis of Related Literature and Studies
Based on the reviewed literature and studies, digital platforms play a significant role in improving supply chain coordination, market efficiency, and sustainability. According to both international and Philippine-based studies, pre-ordering systems reduce food waste, improve income stability, and enhance user convenience. These findings strongly support the development and evaluation of AgriCatch as a digital solution for traditional wet markets such as Trabajo Market in Manila.


















SYSTHESIS AND CONCLUSION 
The reviewed literature and studies show that traditional wet markets commonly face problems such as poor supply coordination, food waste, limited market access, and income instability. According to various authors, these issues are caused by the lack of structured systems that rely on estimated rather than confirmed demand, which is similar to the situation observed in Trabajo Market.
The literature further indicates that digital platforms, particularly pre-ordering systems, help improve coordination between producers, vendors, and consumers. According to supply chain and market efficiency theories, access to accurate and timely information reduces oversupply and shortages while improving product availability. These findings support the use of AgriCatch as a web-based solution for organizing market transactions.
In addition, studies based on the Technology Acceptance Model emphasize that perceived usefulness and ease of use influence users’ willingness to adopt digital systems. This supports the study’s focus on evaluating the acceptability of AgriCatch among vendors and consumers. ICT for Development and sustainability studies also highlight that digital platforms can improve market access and reduce food waste, especially for small-scale farmers and fishermen.
Overall, the reviewed literature and studies provide strong support for evaluating AgriCatch as a practical and acceptable digital solution for a traditional wet market. These findings justify the assessment of the system’s acceptability and perceived effectiveness within the defined scope and delimitation of the study.
